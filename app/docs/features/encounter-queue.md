# Encounter queue

**Purpose.** The encounter screen holds a queue of combatants that the GM adds from the adventure's base entities, each showing its image, numbered name and HP, so the GM sets up a fight's order and HP on the encounter itself and runs the fight's HP there, instead of tracking them outside the app. This page covers the first five of six slices of the encounter screen (`_product/epics/encounter-screen.md`): the first built the combat tables and the queue on them, the second added selection and an Actions tab with each action stored as a combat log entry, the third added the Log tab, which lists the fight's combat log with times, reverts automatic entries and takes typed messages with @mentions of entities, and the fourth added multi-select: Cmd/Ctrl-click and Shift-click selection in the queue, a drag of the selection as one block, and group actions with a different amount per target, each written as one log entry, and the fifth shows the clicked combatant in the middle column with its editable combat name, read-only HP, own notes and its entity's editable content sections and description. Ad-hoc combatants and encounter events follow in the last slice, and no release ships before all six are merged.

**Users.** The GM, as everywhere in the app.

## How it works

Happy path:

1. The encounter screen shows three columns. The left column holds the queue with Hide defeated, Duplicate and Delete below it. The middle column holds the global combat notes at the top and, below them, the shown combatant's view. The right column is a sidebar with an Add tab, an Actions tab and a Log tab, drawn like browser tabs; the Add tab is open when the encounter opens.
2. The Add tab lists the adventure's base entities of every type by name, A to Z, each row with the entity's image (the image placeholder when it has none), its name, its type label, a quantity field and an Add button. Six boxes (NPC, Foe, PC, Faction, Location, Item) filter the list to the checked types, and none checked lists every type. The choice is saved on this device and survives leaving the encounter, opening another encounter and restarting the app. The search box narrows the list by name and description, name matches first.
3. The quantity starts at 1 and takes whole numbers from 1 to 99. Add puts that many combatants of the entity at the end of the queue and sets the quantity back to 1.
4. A combatant shows the entity's name. While an entity has one combatant in the encounter it reads "Goblin"; once a second exists both are numbered ("Goblin 1", "Goblin 2"), and further adds continue from the highest number the entity has had in the encounter, removed combatants included. A removed combatant does not count towards the numbering of the others, so removing "Goblin 2" leaves the other one reading "Goblin" and the next Add shows "Goblin 1" and "Goblin 3". A combatant of an unnamed entity reads "Untitled Combatant". A combatant the GM renamed shows its own name instead (step 24).
5. When the entity has exactly one stat block section (Basic or 5e) whose HP is a number, each new combatant starts with that number as its max and current HP, copied at the moment of the add. A queue entry shows `current / max`, followed by `+temp` when temp HP is above 0, and the entity's image beside them.
6. A combatant that has lost HP has its image tinted red from the bottom up, covering the share of max HP that is missing; at current HP 0 or below, or at max HP 0 or below, the image turns grey instead. A defeated combatant's image is grey whatever its HP, in place of the red tint, and unmarking brings back its colours and the tint its HP gives; the entry is not dimmed. A combatant whose HP is not set shows no HP text and no tint.
7. Dragging a queue entry by its handle moves it to the drop position among the shown entries, and the new order survives leaving the encounter and restarting the app. A selected entry moves with the whole selection as one block, keeping the order of its members; an unselected entry moves alone and leaves the selection unchanged. While a block is held, the other selected entries disappear from their places and the dragged entry shows a "+N" badge, N the number of other selected entries; on release all of them land together. The entry, or the block, lands directly before or after the shown entry it is dropped on, and every hidden or removed combatant keeps its order relative to the entries that did not move, so a hidden one reappears among them as it was. A drag that is cancelled, or dropped where it started, changes nothing.
8. Renaming a base entity changes the shown name of its combatants in every encounter, except a combatant the GM renamed, which keeps its own name. Deleting a base entity keeps its combatants, shown with the name the entity had when they were added, or their own name when the GM renamed them, and the image placeholder.
9. Each Add also writes one `added` entry to the encounter's combat log naming the queue items it added.
10. A click on a queue entry selects only it and shows it in the middle column: the entry gets the app's active highlight; when the sidebar is on the Add tab it switches to Actions, and from the Actions or the Log tab it stays where it is. A Cmd-click (Ctrl-click on Windows and Linux; either key works on every platform) adds an unselected entry to the selection and shows it, or removes a selected one, and removing the last one leaves nothing selected; removing the shown entry leaves nothing shown, and removing another entry leaves the shown entry as it is. A Shift-click selects exactly the shown entries from the anchor to the clicked entry, both included, shows the clicked entry and deselects every other entry; the anchor is the entry last clicked without Shift, a further Shift-click ranges from the same anchor, Shift wins when Cmd or Ctrl is also held, and a range never includes a combatant hidden by Hide defeated. A Shift-click with no anchor, or with its anchor removed or hidden, selects only the clicked entry and makes it the anchor. Every one of these clicks moves the Add tab to Actions and leaves the Actions and Log tabs where they are, a Cmd-click that leaves nothing selected included. Escape clears the selection, the anchor and the shown entry, also when the same press closes a dialog, except while focus is in an editable element of the middle column (the name field, the notes, a content section, the description), where it clears none of them. Reopening the encounter starts with nothing selected and nothing shown. A combatant that is removed, or hidden by Hide defeated, leaves the selection and is no longer shown, while the others stay selected.
11. With nothing selected the Actions tab reads "Select a combatant in the queue.". With combatants selected it shows the buttons at the top: Damage, Heal, Set temp HP and Set max HP, and below those Mark defeated, Unmark defeated and Remove. Below the buttons is one row per selected combatant in queue order, each with the shown name, its HP as the queue shows it ("HP is not set. Set max HP to track it." when HP is not set) and its own amount field. With two or more selected, a shared amount field sits above the buttons; with one selected there is none. The tab fills the sidebar panel and only the row list scrolls, so the shared field and the buttons stay in view.
12. An empty row field shows the placeholder "Amount" and the shared field "Amount for all", and each field is wide enough to show its placeholder in full. Every amount field takes whole numbers of 0 or more, and `1e3` counts as 1000. Typing in the shared field fills every row's field with that text, and any row can then be changed on its own. Damage and Heal apply to the selected combatants whose HP is set and skip the others, and are disabled when none has HP set; Set temp HP and Set max HP apply to every selected combatant. Each of the four amount buttons is disabled while a row it applies to holds an empty or invalid amount; a skipped row's amount never blocks. Enter in a field applies nothing. After an action every row keeps its amount and the shared field its text, so the same amounts can be applied again. A row keeps its own amount while its combatant stays selected, and a combatant that joins the selection starts with the shared text. With one combatant selected its row's amount is the shared amount, so it carries over to the next single combatant selected.
13. Damage takes temp HP first and the rest from current HP, and current HP can go below 0: `4 / 7 +2` with Damage 5 becomes `1 / 7`. Heal raises current HP up to max HP, never above, and leaves temp HP as it is. Set temp HP replaces the temp HP, and 0 clears it. Set max HP changes max HP for this encounter only: when HP was not set, current HP becomes the new max; when the new max is below current HP, current HP drops to it; raising it leaves current HP as it is. The entity's stat block is never changed.
14. Mark defeated turns the image of every selected combatant that is not defeated grey whatever its HP, and Unmark defeated brings back the colours and the tint its HP gives for every selected combatant that is defeated. Mark defeated is disabled when all selected combatants are defeated and Unmark defeated when none is. No HP value sets or clears the mark.
15. Remove takes every selected combatant out of the queue at once, without a confirmation, and clears the selection.
16. Each action writes one entry to the encounter's combat log when its button is clicked, without the autosave delay: a `damage`, `heal`, `temp-hp`, `max-hp`, `defeated`, `undefeated` or `removed` entry that lists every combatant the action changed and, where it takes one, the amount each got. A group action is one entry, so it applies to all its targets or none, and the Log tab shows it as one line, such as "Damage: Goblin 1 (5), Goblin 2 (3)". The queue's HP, defeated marks and removals are derived from those entries, so they survive leaving the encounter and restarting the app.
17. A Hide defeated box under the queue, above Duplicate and Delete, hides defeated combatants while it is checked. A hidden combatant keeps its place and reappears there when the box is unchecked. The screen changes on the click and the write follows about 500 ms later.
18. The Log tab lists every entry of the encounter's combat log, oldest at the top. It opens only on a click on its tab: an action, a selection or an Add never switches to it. The list is scrolled to the newest entry when the tab opens and whenever a new entry appears, and with no entries it reads "No entries yet.". Every entry shows the time it was made at its right, as hour and minute in 12-hour format ("2:32 PM"), without a date.
19. An automatic entry is a compact muted line that reads its label first and then every target its entry holds, comma-separated, each with its amount or value in brackets: "Added: Goblin ×5" ("Added: Goblin" for one, the name without its number), "Damage: Goblin 3 (5)", "Heal: Goblin 3 (4)", "Temp HP: Aria (5)", "Max HP: Goblin 3 (12)", "Defeated: Goblin 3", "Undefeated: Goblin 3", "Removed: Goblin 3" and "Reverted: Damage: Goblin 3 (5)". A line names a combatant as the queue shows it now, so a rename, an entity rename or a number dropped after a sibling's removal changes how earlier lines read. A removed combatant is named as if it were in the queue beside the remaining combatants of its key, so it reads "Goblin 2" while the queue shows "Goblin"; when no sibling of its key remains, it is numbered among every combatant of its key, so two removed Goblins read "Goblin 1" and "Goblin 2".
20. While the pointer is over a revertible entry (Damage, Heal, Temp HP, Max HP, Defeated, Undefeated or Removed) that is not yet reverted, a Revert button shows; one click writes a `revert` entry at once, without a confirmation. The fight's values become what they would be had the entry never happened, and every later entry stays in effect: a combatant at `7 / 7` with Damage 5 then Heal 4 shows `6 / 7`, and reverting the Damage shows `7 / 7`. The reverted entry stays in the list, struck through and muted, without a Revert button, and a "Reverted: …" line is added at the bottom. An entry is reverted once; `Added` entries, `Reverted` lines and typed messages have no Revert. A revert drops the whole entry, every target it lists included. Reverting a Removed entry brings every listed combatant back into the queue at its old place with the HP, defeated mark and number the remaining entries give, and none of them is selected.
21. A composer sits at the bottom of the Log tab with the placeholder "Write a message...". It takes text, line breaks and @mentions of entities, as the notes editor's @ list offers them, and has no formatting toolbar, slash commands, lists or headings; a combatant such as "Goblin 3" is plain text, only its entity can be mentioned. Enter sends the message as a `message` entry, Shift+Enter starts a new line, and Enter while the @ list is open picks the highlighted entity and sends nothing. An empty message, or one of spaces only, is not sent, and a message that holds only a mention is. After a send the composer is empty, and the message is the newest entry, shown as a chat bubble in normal text colour with its mentions as the notes editor shows them; a click on a mention opens the entity's mention popup. A refused send keeps the typed text.
22. While the pointer is over a typed message, a Delete button shows; one click removes the message at once, without a confirmation. A message cannot be edited, and an automatic entry cannot be deleted.
23. With nothing shown, the middle column reads "Click a combatant in the queue to show it here." below the global notes.
24. With a combatant shown, the middle column below the global notes starts with a name field showing the name the queue shows ("Goblin 3"). Typing another name renames this combatant only: the queue entry, its Actions tab row and every Log line naming that one combatant show the typed name, without a number, instantly as the GM types, and the database write follows about 0.5 s later. The "Added" line keeps the entity's name ("Added: Goblin ×5"). Emptying the field, a name of spaces only, or typing exactly the automatic name returns the combatant to its automatic name, numbered as before. Leaving the field or pressing Enter restores the shown name in the field. A renamed combatant still counts for its siblings' numbers: renaming "Goblin 1" to "Boss" leaves the other showing "Goblin 2". A rename writes no log entry.
25. Below the name the HP shows read-only as the queue shows it (`4 / 7`, `4 / 7 +2`), followed by a muted "Defeated" label while the combatant is marked defeated. It changes only through the Actions tab. A combatant whose HP is not set shows a sentence instead: "HP is not set: the entity has no stat block with an HP value. Set max HP in the Actions tab." when its entity has no stat block section or its one stat block has an empty HP; "HP is not set: the entity has more than one stat block. Set max HP in the Actions tab." when it has two or more; "HP is not set. Set max HP in the Actions tab." otherwise, when the stat block changed after the add or the entity was deleted. The entity's sections are read now, not at the moment of the add, and while they load a linked combatant shows no sentence.
26. Below the HP a notes editor with the placeholder "Combatant notes..." takes text and @mentions as the app's other notes editors do. Its text belongs to this combatant only: Goblin 3's notes are not Goblin 1's and do not appear on the Goblin entity. A note writes no log entry.
27. Below the notes, a combatant whose entity exists shows the entity's content section panel and description editor, editable exactly as on the entity's screen: adding, renaming, deleting and stepping through sections, editing a section's body, editing the description. An edit there changes the entity itself, so the entity's screen, its mention popup and every combatant of that entity, in this and other encounters, show it. A combatant whose entity was deleted shows its name, HP, defeated label and notes, and no entity view.
28. The global notes keep their fixed-height panel at the top. Everything below it, from the name field to the end of the description, scrolls as one area: the combatant notes and the description grow with their text, and the content section panel shows unchanged, as on the entity screen, so a section body keeps its own height and inner scroll.

Failure:

- A quantity outside 1 to 99, or not a whole number, disables Add, and leaving the field puts back the last valid quantity.
- While an Add of the encounter is pending, every Add button of the encounter is disabled; a second Add call made in the same moment is refused, and a refused Add leaves the typed quantity as it was.
- A group action is one log write, so it applies to all its targets or to none, and a target the action cannot change is left out of its entry.
- While an action write of the encounter is pending, every action button is disabled, and stays so until the queue shows the new numbers; a second action call made in the same moment is refused and writes nothing.
- A quit within about 500 ms of clicking Hide defeated loses that one toggle, since it saves through the encounter's autosave. A quit within about 500 ms of the last keystroke in the name field or the notes loses that edit the same way.
- If the `added` log entry cannot be written after the combatants were stored, the Add still counts as done and the error is swallowed, so a retry cannot add the same batch twice. The log entry is then missing.
- A failing read or write of the queue or the log shows the app's error screen, as everywhere in the app. A log entry that cannot be read — an unknown kind, invalid JSON or a payload that fails its schema — applies nothing and never stops the others, a target without a queue item is skipped, and the Log tab shows the entry as "Unreadable entry" with its time and no Revert.
- While a log write of the encounter is pending — an action, a Revert, a Delete or a send — every action button, every Revert and Delete button and sending are blocked: the buttons are disabled until the queue shows the new numbers, and a second call made in the same moment is refused and writes nothing. A refused send keeps the typed text.
- The Log tab leaves out a target that has no queue item on this device and shows only the label when none is left, and it shows a `Reverted` entry whose target the derivation ignored as "Unreadable entry"; both arise only mid-sync and fix themselves once the sync completes, and neither throws.
- An adventure without base entities shows "This adventure has no entities yet." in the Add tab; a search without a match shows "No results found".

Where the user waits: during a block drag the other selected entries leave the list one frame after the drag starts; the Add tab shows the loading icon until the adventure's entities have loaded; the Log tab shows the loading icon in its list area until the queue, the log and the entities have loaded, and its composer is usable at once; the screen shows the loading icon it showed before while the encounter loads. A drag reorder shows its new order at once, ahead of the write. After an action the buttons stay disabled until the queue shows the new numbers, which is the write plus the refetch of the log.

## Data

- **Reads:** the encounter's queue items; the adventure's base entities of all six types, from the same cached lists the entity list screens use, so any change to an entity refreshes the queue and the Add tab; the encounter's log entries, oldest first, which the Log tab lists and the queue is derived from; the encounter's `hide_defeated`; an entity's content sections, at the moment of an Add for the stat block HP and while a combatant is shown for the HP-not-set sentence; the per-device setting `encounterAddFilter`.
- **Writes:** one batch of queue items per Add, each with the encounter, the kind `combatant`, the entity, its name at that time, its sequence number, its starting max HP when the stat block gave one, and its position in the queue; one `added` entry in the combat log, its payload the list of queue item ids; one log entry per action, its kind and its payload, which lists every target the action changed (`damage` and `heal` store `targets` of queue item id and `amount`, `temp-hp` and `max-hp` store `targets` of queue item id and `value`, `defeated`, `undefeated` and `removed` store `queueItemIds`), and the entries the Log tab writes: a `revert` entry per Revert, which stores the `entryId` it reverts, and a `message` entry per send, which stores the `editorState` (the serialised editor state) of the typed message; the deletion of a typed message, which removes its log row and is limited to `message` rows, so the rest of the log is never deleted; the encounter's `hide_defeated`; the rename (`custom_name`) and the notes of a queue item, each through the autosave path with no log entry, and edits to the shown entity's content sections and description through the entity's own writes; a new position for every queue item on a reorder, the hidden and removed ones keeping their places; the setting `encounterAddFilter`, a list of entity types.
- **Columns with no reader yet:** the kind `event` on the queue items exists from the first slice's migration on, because the epic has one migration; a later slice reads it.
- **Sync:** the queue items and the log entries sync to paired devices as other tables do, listed after the encounters. The setting is not synced. Duplicating an encounter does not copy its queue in this slice, and the copy takes the source's `hide_defeated`. A deleted typed message syncs as a deletion, so it disappears from a paired device. Deleting an encounter deletes its queue items and log entries; deleting a base entity empties the entity link of its queue items.
- **Derived, not stored:** a combatant's max, current and temp HP, its defeated mark and its removal are derived from its queue item and the encounter's log entries, so no resulting value is stored, and which entries a revert dropped are derived the same way; the shown name is derived from the live entity, and from `custom_name` when it is set.

This feature adds a migration, which gives the build a new migration head, and a peer counts as compatible only when its sync protocol version and its migration head both equal the device's own (`app/services/syncService.ts:76-77`; decision record `0001-encounter-combat-state.md` — Data, Elsewhere). The sync protocol version itself stays at 3.
